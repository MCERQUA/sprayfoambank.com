A vague, single-number quote makes it impossible to compare offers or catch a bad deal. Here's how to read a spray foam quote the way someone evaluating a loan application would — line by line, with specific questions.

## What Should Be Itemized

A legitimate quote should specify: the foam type (open-cell or closed-cell), the target thickness (and therefore R-value), the total board feet of coverage, any prep work included (substrate cleaning, moisture remediation, masking), and a separate line for labor versus material if the contractor breaks it out that way. A single bottom-line number with no breakdown makes it impossible to know what you're actually comparing between quotes.

## The Thickness Number Is Non-Negotiable

If a quote doesn't specify a target thickness, ask for it directly — this is the single number that most directly determines both your R-value and your total board-foot cost. "We'll spray until it looks right" is not an acceptable answer to a direct thickness question.

## Comparing Two Quotes Fairly

Two quotes for "the same job" that specify different thicknesses or different foam types aren't actually comparable, even if the bottom-line numbers look similar. Normalize both quotes to the same foam type and thickness before comparing price — otherwise you're comparing two different products, not two prices for the same work.

## Red Flags in Pricing Structure

A quote significantly below the range you're seeing elsewhere for the same specified scope deserves a direct question about how they're achieving that price — sometimes there's a reasonable answer, sometimes it reflects thinner-than-specified application or corners cut on prep work. A quote with vague or missing thickness and foam-type specification is itself a red flag, independent of the price.

## What to Ask Before Signing

Ask specifically: what thickness are you targeting, how will you verify it was achieved, what's included in prep work, and what does the written warranty cover. A contractor who answers these specifically and confidently is demonstrating the same rigor you'd want from anyone handling a five-figure home investment.
