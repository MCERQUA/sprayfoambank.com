Financing makes a spray foam project accessible without a large upfront payment — but the terms matter as much as the headline offer. Here are five questions worth asking before you sign anything.

## 1. What Happens When the Promotional Period Ends?

Many 0% or low-interest offers are promotional-period financing, meaning the rate changes — sometimes significantly — once that window closes. Some "deferred interest" plans retroactively charge interest on the full original amount if it's not paid off by the deadline, not just going forward. Ask specifically what the rate becomes and whether any deferred interest applies.

## 2. Is There a Prepayment Penalty?

If you plan to pay off the loan early (from savings, a bonus, or refinancing), confirm there's no penalty for doing so. Most consumer financing doesn't have one, but it's worth a direct question rather than an assumption.

## 3. What's the Total Cost Over the Full Term?

A monthly payment can look manageable while the total cost over the loan's full term is meaningfully higher than the project's cash price. Ask for — or calculate — the total amount you'll pay over the life of the loan, not just the advertised monthly figure.

## 4. Who Actually Holds the Loan?

Contractor-arranged financing is typically originated by a third-party lending partner, not the contractor themselves. Know who you're actually borrowing from, since that's who you'll be dealing with for the life of the loan, not the contractor who arranged the introduction.

## 5. Does Financing Affect the Manufacturer Warranty or Tax Credit Eligibility?

Financing the project generally doesn't affect product warranty coverage or tax credit eligibility, since those are tied to the installation and documentation, not how you paid for it — but it's a reasonable question to confirm directly with both your contractor and your tax professional, especially if the financing structure is unusual.

## The Bottom Line

None of this means financing is a bad idea — for many homeowners, it's the difference between doing the project now versus years from now. It means treating a financing agreement with the same scrutiny you'd apply to any loan, regardless of how the offer is packaged.
